## Numbers and Narrative

## Growth and Value

- **Crecimiento: dos fuerzas** — *El crecimiento de los beneficios se descompone en inversión en activos nuevos (crecimiento sostenible) y mejora de eficiencia sobre los activos existentes.* “we trace earnings growth back to two forces: investment in new assets, also called sustainable growth, and improving efficiency on existing assets, which we term efficiency growth.” (Growth and Value, p. 2)
- **Crecimiento y valor en el modelo de dividendos** — *Más crecimiento no implica más valor si viene con menor pago o mayor riesgo.* “However, if higher earnings growth is accompanied by lower payout ratios and/or higher risk (cost of equity), it is conceivable that it could lead to lower value.” (Growth and Value, p. 5)
- **Crecimiento y valor en el modelo de empresa** — *El mayor crecimiento solo aumenta el valor si se mantienen fijos la tasa de reinversión y el coste de capital.* “Higher expected growth in after-tax operating income will increase the value of a business, if you hold the reinvestment rate and cost of capital fixed.” (Growth and Value, p. 7)
- **Crecimiento como relato en valoración relativa** — *Un mayor crecimiento no basta para justificar un múltiplo más alto: intervienen el riesgo y la calidad del crecimiento.* “First, while higher growth should be (and usually is) correlated with higher earnings multiples, there are other factors that intervene – higher risk and the quality of growth are two factors that come to mind.” (Growth and Value, p. 8)
- **Proposición 1: subir por la cuenta de resultados** — *El crecimiento tiende a ser mayor cuanto más abajo en la cuenta de resultados (BPA > beneficio neto > resultado operativo).* “growth rates for firms will tend to decrease as you move up the income statement. In other words, earnings per share growth rates will be higher than net income growth rates and net income will grow faster than operating income.” (Growth and Value, p. 14)
- **Tamaño y crecimiento** — *Cuanto más grande es la empresa, menos crece, porque mantener tasas altas es más difícil sobre una base mayor.* “As firms get larger, revenues and earnings grow at lower rates, a phenomenon that can be attributed to two factors. The first is that delivering high growth rates becomes more difficult at higher levels of income (revenue) than at lower levels.” (Growth and Value, p. 16)
- **Proposición 3: escalar es difícil** — *La tasa de crecimiento esperada debe ligarse al tamaño de la empresa y decrecer al aumentar este.* “Scaling up is hard to do. The expected growth rate for a firm should be tied to the size of the firm, with growth rates decreasing as the firm gets larger.” (Growth and Value, p. 17)
- **Higgledy piggledy growth** — *Los estudios clásicos no hallan relación entre el crecimiento de un periodo y el del siguiente.* “examined whether British companies that reported high earnings growth in one five-year period continued to exhibit that growth in the next five-year period and concluded that there was no relationship between growth in the two periods.” (Growth and Value, p. 17)
- **Persistencia en EE. UU. (1951–1997)** — *La mediana del crecimiento de beneficios se parece al PIB, el crecimiento alto es raro y no persiste más que por azar.* “the median growth rate in earnings across firms corresponds closely to growth in Gross Domestic Product (GDP), high growth was uncommon and that there was no persistence in earnings growth beyond chance.” (Growth and Value, p. 17)
- **Matriz de persistencia del beneficio neto** — *Las empresas de mayor crecimiento en un periodo no repiten más de lo que haría el azar.* “For the most part, there is no evidence of persistence. For instance, of the firms that delivered the highest growth between 1997 and 2002, only 17.15% remained in the highest growth class for the 2002-07 time period, not significantly different from the 16.67% you would expect to observe with a completely random process.” (Growth and Value, p. 18)
- **Correlación negativa del crecimiento** — *Tras un alto crecimiento del beneficio es más probable un mal crecimiento posterior (reversión a la media).* “Put more starkly, at least over the two time periods that we examined, firms with high growth rates in earnings in one period are more likely to be underperformers when it comes to growth in the next period.” (Growth and Value, p. 19)
- **Proposición 4: poco valor predictivo del pasado** — *El crecimiento histórico predice mal el crecimiento futuro.* “There is little predictive value in historical growth rates and high growth rates in the past are not indicative of high growth rates in the future.” (Growth and Value, p. 19)
- **Proposición 5: previsiones de analistas** — *Los analistas aciertan algo a corto plazo, pero casi nada en el crecimiento a largo plazo.* “Analyst estimates of earnings (and growth) have some predictive value for short-term earnings forecasts, but are of little or any value for long-term growth forecasts.” (Growth and Value, p. 24)
- **Determinantes del crecimiento: ROI estable** — *Supuesto de partida del análisis: un retorno de la inversión estable, que no cambia de un periodo a otro.* “Consider the simplest scenario, where the ROI is stable and does not change from period to period” (Growth and Value, p. 28)
- **Descomposición del crecimiento: inversión nueva** — *El primer término del crecimiento es el retorno marginal de la nueva inversión por la proporción reinvertida.* “The first term in this equation captures the growth from new investments, determined by the marginal return on those investments and the proportion invested in these investments.” (Growth and Value, p. 28)
- **Única fuente de crecimiento sostenible** — *Con retornos estables sobre los activos existentes, la inversión nueva es la única fuente de crecimiento; y siempre la única a largo plazo.* “for a firm with stable returns on existing assets, this is the only source of growth and for all firms, it the only source of long term sustainable growth.” (Growth and Value, p. 30)
- **Retorno marginal frente a medio** — *El retorno de lo nuevo es marginal; el de lo existente es una media, y pueden diferir mucho.* “The return on existing assets is an average return on a portfolio of investments already made. While we often use the same value for both numbers in valuation, they can be different, in fact, very different in practice.” (Growth and Value, p. 30)
- **Proposición 7: crecimiento fundamental** — *El crecimiento sostenible depende solo de la tasa de reinversión y del retorno de lo reinvertido.* “The long term (and sustainable growth rate) for a firm is a function of only two variables – the proportion of earnings that are invested back into the business (the reinvestment rate) and the returns earned on these investments.” (Growth and Value, p. 31)
- **La misma reinversión rinde distinto** — *La misma tasa de reinversión rinde más crecimiento donde el retorno del capital es mayor.* “the same reinvestment rate provides a much bigger bang for the buck in some companies (with high returns on capital) than it does in others.” (Growth and Value, p. 31)
- **Crecimiento por eficiencia: más potencial con retorno bajo** — *Una mejora del retorno sobre un nivel bajo produce mucho más crecimiento que sobre un nivel alto.* “A firm that improves its return on capital from 5% to 6% will report a 20% growth rate from efficiency in that period, whereas a firm that improves its return on capital from 25% to 26% will generate a 4% growth rate from efficiency in that period.” (Growth and Value, p. 32)
- **Eficiencia: duración finita** — *La eficiencia solo justifica crecimiento durante un tiempo limitado; después se vuelve al crecimiento sostenible.* “You can draw on increased efficiency to justify growth only for finite periods. After all, a firm cannot be infinitely inefficient. Once the inefficiencies, no matter how significant, are fixed, the firm will have to revert back to its sustainable growth rate, based upon new investments.” (Growth and Value, p. 32)
- **Eficiencia en el valor terminal** — *En el periodo de alto crecimiento valen eficiencia e inversión nueva; en el valor terminal, solo la inversión nueva.* “you can draw on both efficiency and new investments to justify growth during the high growth period, but only on new investments to justify growth forever (in the terminal value computation).” (Growth and Value, p. 32)
- **Las ganancias de eficiencia se agotan** — *Cuando se agotan las mejoras de eficiencia, el crecimiento cae hacia el sostenible; no se puede seguir retirando capital.* “As efficiency gains run their course, earnings growth rates will decline towards the sustainable growth rate. In other words, firms cannot continue to draw down capital and expect to keep growth at healthy levels.” (Growth and Value, p. 33)
- **Crecimiento que rebota** — *El crecimiento de las empresas rebota con poca predictibilidad; alto o bajo hoy no anticipa el siguiente periodo.* “Growth rates at companies bounce around, with little predictability. Firms that generate high growth in one period are just as likely to be low growth as high growth firms in the next period.” (Growth and Value, p. 34)
- **El tamaño frena reinversión y retorno** — *Para crecer mucho hay que reinvertir mucho con retorno alto, y el tamaño juega en contra de ambas cosas.* “For a firm to grow at a high rate, it has be able to reinvest a large proportion of its earnings, while maintaining a high return on capital. On both dimensions, size works against companies.” (Growth and Value, p. 34)
- **Decaimiento hacia el crecimiento de la economía** — *Las tasas de crecimiento se desvanecen deprisa hacia las de la economía.* “Across companies, growth rates fade towards that of the economy quickly. For instance, the real revenue growth rate of the companies that were in the S&P 500 in 1969 decreased from 7% in that year to the GDP growth rate of 4% in 1974 and down to 2% in 1979” (Growth and Value, p. 35)
- **Proposición 9: duración del alto crecimiento** — *Crecer por encima de la economía durante diez años o más es la excepción.* “Firms that grow at rates higher than the economy for extended periods (10 years or more) are more the exception than the rule.” (Growth and Value, p. 35)
- **Retorno igual al coste de capital** — *Si el retorno de la nueva inversión iguala el coste del capital, el crecimiento es neutro para el valor.* “In the special case where the return on equity is equal to the cost of equity, growth has a neutral effect on value, with the positive effect of higher growth exactly offset by the negative effect of lower cash flows.” (Growth and Value, p. 37)
- **Versión de empresa: ROIC frente a coste de capital** — *Con ROIC igual, mayor o menor que el coste de capital, crecer no crea, crea o destruye valor.* “A firm that generates a return on capital equal to its cost of capital will create no value as it increases growth, whereas a firm that is able to earn more (less) than its cost of capital will increase (decrease) value as it increases growth.” (Growth and Value, p. 37)
- **Proposición 10: crecimiento con exceso de retorno** — *No es el crecimiento en sí, sino el crecimiento con retornos excedentes positivos el que crea valor.* “It is not growth, per se, that creates value but growth with positive excess returns. Higher growth can add value, destroy value or leave value unchanged.” (Growth and Value, p. 37)

## Measuring Returns

- **Excess returns y valor** — *El crecimiento sin retornos en exceso no crea valor; es el eje de los modelos de valoración recientes.* “more recent iterations of these models have noted that growth unaccompanied by excess returns creates no value.” (Measuring Returns, p. 2)
- **Retorno frente a coste de capital** — *Una empresa que gana más de lo que le cuesta el capital obtiene retornos en exceso y cotiza con prima.* “A firm that generates higher returns on an investment than it costs it to raise capital for that investment is earning excess returns and will trade at a premium over a firm that does not earn excess returns.” (Measuring Returns, p. 5)
- **Crecimiento = reinversión × ROIC** — *El crecimiento esperado de la empresa es la tasa de reinversión por el retorno del capital nuevo.* “In firm valuation models, the expected growth rate is a product of the return on capital invested in new assets and the proportion of operating income reinvested back into the business (reinvestment rate)” (Measuring Returns, p. 5)
- **Qué número manda en la valoración** — *El retorno sobre el capital importa más que el coste de capital.* “we can safely conclude that the key number in a valuation is not the cost of capital that we assign a firm but the return earned on capital that we attribute to it.” (Measuring Returns, p. 6)
- **Definición de ROIC** — *El ROIC mide el retorno sobre el capital invertido en una inversión, con resultado operativo después de impuestos en el numerador.* “The return on capital or invested capital in a business attempts to measure the return earned on capital invested in an investment.” (Measuring Returns, p. 7)
- **Capital del año anterior** — *El denominador es el capital invertido al cierre del año previo y el numerador el resultado operativo del año corriente.* “The final is the timing difference; the capital invested is from the end of the prior year whereas the operating income is the current year’s number.” (Measuring Returns, p. 7)
- **Capital contable y no de mercado** — *Se usa valor contable porque el de mercado incluye activos de crecimiento que aún no generan resultado operativo.* “The market value of equity includes the expected value of growth assets, which cannot generate operating income today.” (Measuring Returns, p. 9)
- **Neto de caja** — *La caja se resta del capital porque su interés no forma parte del resultado operativo.* “The reason we net out cash is to be consistent with the use of operating income as our measure of earnings. The interest income from cash is not part of operating income.” (Measuring Returns, p. 9)
- **Capital al inicio del periodo** — *Las inversiones hechas durante el año no producen resultado ese año, por eso se divide por el capital inicial.* “Using the rationale that investments made during the course of a year will generally not start generating earnings during the year, we divide the operating income for the year by the capital invested at the beginning of the year.” (Measuring Returns, p. 10)
- **ROE frente a ROIC y la caja** — *El ROE contable es un retorno compuesto de todos los activos, caja incluida, a diferencia del ROIC.* “The return on equity for a company is therefore a composite return on all of its assets – cash and operating.” (Measuring Returns, p. 12)
- **Capitalización de I+D: vida útil por sector** — *La vida amortizable de la I+D depende del sector: larga en farmacia, corta en software.* “To illustrate, research and development expenses at a pharmaceutical company should have fairly long amortizable lives, since the approval process for new drugs is long. In contrast, research and development expenses at a software firm, where products tend to emerge from research much more quickly should be amortized over a shorter period.” (Measuring Returns, p. 19)
- **Ajuste del resultado operativo por I+D** — *Se suma el gasto de I+D y se resta la amortización del activo de investigación.* “First, the R&D expenses that were subtracted out to arrive at the operating income are added back to the operating income, reflecting their re-categorization as capital expenses. Next, the amortization of the research asset is treated the same way that depreciation is and netted out to arrive at the adjusted operating income.” (Measuring Returns, p. 20)
- **Efecto de capitalizar I+D** — *Capitalizar I+D tiende a bajar los retornos que parecían altos antes del ajuste.* “Firms that report high returns on a pre-adjustment basis are more likely to see their returns go down, post-adjustment.” (Measuring Returns, p. 21)
- **Arrendamientos operativos como deuda** — *Los arrendamientos operativos son compromisos contractuales equivalentes a deuda y deben tratarse como tal.* “Operating lease commitments look very much like debt commitments insofar as firms as contractually obligated to make them.” (Measuring Returns, p. 31)
- **Partidas no recurrentes** — *El resultado usado para calcular retornos debe reflejar operaciones continuadas, sin partidas únicas o extraordinarias.* “As a general rule, the income that is used to compute returns on equity and capital should reflect continuing operations and should not include any items that are one-time or extraordinary.” (Measuring Returns, p. 37)
- **Recompras y ROE** — *Dividendos y recompras elevan el ROE al reducir el patrimonio contable más que el resultado.* “In general, the return on equity of a firm that pays a large dividend or buys back stock will increase after the transaction because the book value of equity will decrease disproportionately, relative to the net income.” (Measuring Returns, p. 39)
- **Goodwill y activos de crecimiento** — *Del goodwill, solo el valor de los activos de crecimiento debe salir del capital invertido; el resto se queda.* “After all, a company cannot be asked to generate a return on investments it has not thought about yet. The other elements of goodwill, though, should remain part of capital invested.” (Measuring Returns, p. 41)
- **Participaciones minoritarias** — *Las participaciones minoritarias no afectan al resultado operativo, así que su inversión se excluye del capital invertido.* “Minority holdings in other companies will not affect operating income but the investments in these holdings should not be included as part of invested capital.” (Measuring Returns, p. 46)
- **Volatilidad del ROE frente al ROIC** — *El ROE es más volátil que el retorno sobre capital porque el interés es un coste fijo.* “The returns on equity are more volatile than the returns on capital: Equity earnings will be more volatile than operating earnings, largely because interest expenses comprise a fixed cost.” (Measuring Returns, p. 49)
- **Retorno marginal frente a medio** — *Las empresas invierten primero en lo mejor, así que el retorno marginal suele quedar por debajo del medio.* “If we begin with the proposition that firms invest in their best projects first and move down their investment schedule to less and less attractive investments, the marginal return on capital (or equity) should be lower than the average return for most firms.” (Measuring Returns, p. 51)
- **Dell: el medio engaña** — *Un retorno medio alto no implica que las inversiones nuevas ganen lo mismo.* “Assuming that Dell has significant new investments that will continue to earn these returns would be not only foolhardy but lead to too high an estimate of value for the company.” (Measuring Returns, p. 52)
- **Competencia erosiona el exceso** — *Los retornos en exceso atraen competidores que los presionan a la baja.* “While this excess return may be justified using historical data or industry averages, the presence of these returns will undoubtedly draw in new competitors over time, putting downward pressure on these returns over time.” (Measuring Returns, p. 56)
- **Competencia perfecta** — *En competencia perfecta el exceso desaparece de inmediato; hacen falta barreras para que persista.* “In a perfectly competitive market place, excess returns will not persist for more than an instant in time and all firms will earn zero excess returns.” (Measuring Returns, p. 56)
- **Ventaja competitiva duradera** — *Cuanto más fuerte y sostenible la ventaja, mayor y más largo el retorno en exceso.* “Competitive advantages that are strong and sustainable generate larger excess returns for longer periods than more fleeting or weaker competitive advantages.” (Measuring Returns, p. 57)
- **Retornos más persistentes que el crecimiento** — *El crecimiento revierte rápido a la media; el ROIC puede mantenerse alto mucho tiempo.* “The McKinsey study suggests that while revenue growth tends to revert quickly to average levels, returns on invested capital can remain high for extended periods.” (Measuring Returns, p. 59)
- **Horizonte de la ventaja** — *La duración del periodo de exceso puede venir fijada por la vida explícita de la ventaja, como una patente.* “In some cases, the length of the short term and transition periods will be determined by the fact that the competitive advantage has an explicit life.” (Measuring Returns, p. 60)
- **ROIC = coste de capital en estabilidad** — *Si el retorno iguala el coste de capital, subir el crecimiento estable no cambia el valor.* “If the return on capital is equal to the cost of capital, increasing the stable growth rate will have no effect on value.” (Measuring Returns, p. 61)
- **Tope del exceso perpetuo** — *Las empresas maduras ganan como mucho 2-3 puntos sobre su coste de capital a largo plazo; ese es el techo.* “At best, mature firms have earned 2-3% over their cost of capital over long periods and this should represent a cap on excess returns in perpetuity.” (Measuring Returns, p. 63)
- **Suelo del retorno terminal** — *En el valor terminal el retorno no debe quedar por debajo del coste de capital.* “Thus, it seems prudent to assume that the return on capital (equity) will, at the minimum, be equal to the cost of capital (equity) in the terminal value calculation.” (Measuring Returns, p. 63)
- **Conclusión: los excesos decrecen** — *Los retornos altos pueden durar unos años, pero decaen con el tamaño y la competencia.* “Thus, firms that have generated high returns in the past may continue to make these returns for the next few years, but the excess returns will start decreasing as firms become larger and competition increases.” (Measuring Returns, p. 64)

## Closure in Valuation

- **Cierre de la valoración** — *Como no se pueden estimar flujos para siempre, se corta la proyección y se calcula un valor terminal.* “Since you cannot estimate cash flows forever, you generally impose closure in discounted cash flow valuation by stopping your estimation of cash flows sometime in the future and then computing a terminal value that reflects the value of the firm at that point.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 1)
- **Crecimiento estable** — *Con el tamaño, el crecimiento acaba igualando como máximo al de la economía.* “As a firm grows, it becomes more difficult for it to maintain high growth and it eventually will grow at a rate less than or equal to the growth rate of the economy in which it operates.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 1)
- **Valor de liquidación** — *Alternativa al negocio en marcha: la empresa cesa y vende sus activos.* “In some valuations, we can assume that the firm will cease operations at a point in time in the future and sell the assets it has accumulated to the highest bidders. The estimate that emerges is called a liquidation value.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 2)
- **Múltiplos como valor terminal** — *Usar múltiplos de comparables mezcla valoración relativa y por flujos descontados.* “All in all, using multiples to estimate terminal value, when those multiples are estimated from comparable firms, results in a dangerous mix of relative and discounted cash flow valuation.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 3)
- **Valor terminal de Gordon (I3)** — *Valor terminal = flujo del año siguiente ÷ (r − g estable), con flujo y tasa de empresa o de capital propio según el caso.* “If we assume that cash flows, beyond the terminal year, will grow at a constant rate forever, the terminal value can be estimated as.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 3)
- **Sensibilidad a g estable** — *Ningún dato pesa más en el valor que la tasa de crecimiento estable.* “Of all the inputs into a discounted cash flow valuation model, none can affect the value more than the stable growth rate.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 4)
- **Límite de g estable (E1)** — *La g constante no puede superar el crecimiento de la economía.* “Since no firm can grow forever at a rate higher than the growth rate of the economy in which it operates, the constant growth rate cannot be greater than the overall growth rate of the economy.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 4)
- **g por debajo de la tasa de descuento (I3, E1)** — *Fijar g ≤ crecimiento de la economía garantiza además que g sea menor que la tasa de descuento.* “Setting the stable growth rate to be less than or equal to the growth rate of the economy is not only the consistent thing to do but it also ensures that the growth rate will be less than the discount rate.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 5)
- **Regla práctica con la tasa sin riesgo (E1)** — *Como regla práctica, g estable no debe superar la tasa sin riesgo usada en la valoración.* “In fact, a simple rule of thumb on the stable growth rate is that it should not exceed the riskless rate used in the valuation.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 5)
- **Tres supuestos de la fase estable** — *Cuándo llega la estabilidad, cómo es la empresa entonces y cómo se hace la transición.* “The first relates to when the firm that you are valuing will become a stable growth firm, if it is not one already. The second relates to what the characteristics of the firm will be in stable growth” (Closure in Valuation, p. 6)
- **El muro del crecimiento estable** — *Todas las empresas llegan a la estabilidad porque el tamaño frena el crecimiento alto.* “All firms ultimately become stable growth firms, in the best case, because high growth makes a firm larger and the firm’s size will eventually become a barrier to further high growth.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 6)
- **Los excesos de retorno se erosionan** — *La competencia acaba eliminando los retornos extraordinarios.* “In a competitive market, these excess returns will eventually draw in new competitors and the excess returns will disappear.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 7)
- **Duración del crecimiento alto: tamaño** — *Las empresas pequeñas en mercados grandes sostienen más tiempo el crecimiento y los excesos de retorno.* “Smaller firms are much more likely to earn excess returns and maintain these excess returns than otherwise similar larger firms. This is because they have more room to grow and a larger potential market.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 7)
- **Duración del crecimiento alto: ventaja y barreras** — *La ventaja competitiva sostenible y las barreras de entrada son el factor más decisivo de la duración.* “This is perhaps the most critical determinant of the length of the high growth period. If there are significant barriers to entry and sustainable competitive advantages, firms can maintain high growth for longer periods.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 7)
- **Ejemplo de duración (Amgen)** — *La duración se juzga con patentes y mercado en expansión; es un juicio subjetivo (ejemplo del paper: diez años).* “The patents that Amgen has will protect it from competition and the long lead time to drug approval will ensure that new products will take a while getting to the market. We will allow for ten years of high growth and excess returns.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 9)
- **Periodo de ventaja competitiva** — *El valor es el capital invertido más el valor actual de los excesos de retorno; sin excesos después, no se añade valor.* “The value of such a firm can then be written as the sum of the capital invested today and the present value of the excess returns that the firm will earn over its life.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 9)
- **Cómo cambia la empresa al estabilizarse** — *Menos riesgo, más deuda, menos exceso de retorno y menos reinversión.* “In general, you would expect stable growth firms to be less risky, use more debt, have lower (or even no) excess returns and reinvest less than high growth firms.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 9)
- **ROC estable: sin exceso en teoría (E2, E3)** — *Hay quien sostiene que lo coherente es ROC igual al coste de capital.* “There are some who believe that the only assumption consistent with stable growth is to assume no excess returns; the return on capital is set equal to the cost of capital.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 10)
- **ROC estable: excesos en la práctica (E2)** — *En la práctica no se pierden de golpe los excesos; el ROC converge hacia los promedios del sector.* “While, in principle, excess returns in perpetuity are not feasible, it is difficult in practice to assume that firms will suddenly lose the capacity to earn excess returns.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 10)
- **ROC hacia la media del sector (E2)** — *Llevar ROE y ROC hacia los promedios del sector da valoraciones más razonables.* “assuming a firm’s returns on equity and capital will move towards industry averages will yield more reasonable estimates of value.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 11)
- **Reinversión en estado estable** — *Hay que reducir la reinversión por el menor crecimiento y a la vez asegurar que sostenga la g estable.* “Stable growth firms tend to reinvest less than high growth firms and it is critical that we both capture the effects of lower growth on reinvestment and that we ensure that the firm reinvests enough to sustain its stable growth rate in the terminal phase.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 12)
- **Retención = g ÷ ROE (ejemplo)** — *La retención (o reinversión de capital propio) es g dividido por ROE; ejemplo del paper con 5% y 15%.* “Procter & Gamble will have to reinvest 33.33% of its earnings into the firm to generate its expected growth of 5%; it can pay out the remaining 66.67%.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 12)
- **Tasa de reinversión estable (I2)** — *Reinversión en estado estable = g estable ÷ ROC estable (la fórmula impresa en p. 13 sale aplanada y ambigua; ver trazabilidad).* “where the ROCn is the return on capital that the firm can sustain in stable growth. This reinvestment rate can then be used to generate the free cash flow to the firm in the first year of stable growth.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 13)
- **Vincular reinversión y g estable (E3)** — *Si ROC = coste de capital, subir la g estable no cambia el valor; si ROC es mayor, lo sube.* “If the return on capital is higher than the cost of capital in the stable growth period, increasing the stable growth rate will increase value. If the return on capital is equal to the cost of captial, increasing the stable growth rate will have no effect on value.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 13)
- **Ejemplo Alloy Mills: g no cambia el valor (E3)** — *Con ROC = 10% = coste de capital, bajar g de 5% a 0% deja el valor en 1.300 (cifras del paper, solo ejemplo).* “The value of the firm remains unchanged at $1,300 million. In fact, changing the stable growth rate to 0% has no effect on value:” (Closure in Valuation, p. 15)
- **Excesos de retorno en la fase estable** — *Poner el ROC por encima del coste de capital supone excesos perpetuos; se acepta moverlos hacia el sector.* “For all of the firms, it is worth noting that you are assuming that excess returns continue in perpetuity by setting the return on capital above the cost of capital.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 17)
- **Modelo de dos fases frente a tres** — *El salto abrupto solo vale con crecimiento moderado; con crecimiento muy alto hace falta fase de transición.* “Since the firm goes in one year from high growth to stable growth in the two-stage model, this model is more appropriate for firms with moderate growth rates, where the shift will not be too dramatic.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 18)
- **Peso del valor terminal** — *Que el valor terminal sea gran parte del valor total es normal, y mayor cuanto más potencial de crecimiento.* “It is true that a large portion of the value of any stock or equity in a business comes from the terminal value, but it would be surprising if were not so.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 23)
- **Peso del valor terminal y crecimiento** — *A más potencial de crecimiento, mayor la proporción del valor que viene del terminal.* “Consequently, the greater the growth potential in a business, the higher the proportion of the value that comes from the terminal value will be.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 23)
- **El valor lo mueven los excesos, no la g** — *Si los excesos de retorno son cero, cambiar g estable no afecta al valor.* “In fact, as we showed earlier in the chapter, it is not the stable growth rate that drives value as much as what we assume about excess returns in perpetuity. When excess returns are zero, changes in the stable growth rate have no impact on value.” (Closure in Valuation, p. 23)

## Probabilistic Approaches

- **Mejor/peor caso: factibilidad** — *Si cada input se lleva a su extremo a la vez, el caso puede no ser factible; hay que respetar las relaciones entre inputs.* “The problem with this approach is that it may not be feasible; after all, to get the high revenue growth, the firm may have to lower prices and accept lower margins.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 2)
- **Mejor/peor caso: riesgo de quiebra** — *El peor caso sirve para juzgar si una inversión podría llevar a la empresa con deuda a incumplir.* “Thus, a firm that has significant debt obligations may use the worst-case outcome to make a judgment as to whether an investment has the potential to push them into default.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 2)
- **Mejor/peor caso: utilidad limitada** — *Un rango muy amplio entre mejor y peor caso no ayuda a decidir si el precio actual es razonable.* “In general, though, best case/worse case analyses are not very informative. After all, there should be no surprise in knowing that an asset will be worth a lot in the best case and not very much in the worst case.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 2)
- **Escenarios: pocos factores** — *Los escenarios se construyen sobre dos o tres factores críticos.* “In general, analysts should focus on the two or three most critical factors that will determine the value of the asset and build scenarios around these factors.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 3)
- **Escenarios: cuántos** — *Pocos escenarios son más fáciles de estimar que muchos; el número depende de cuán distintos son y de cuánto se pueden prever sus flujos.* “Thus, estimating cash flows under each scenario will be easier if the firm lays out five scenarios, for instance, than if it lays out 15 scenarios.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 3)
- **Escenarios: probabilidades y cobertura** — *Las probabilidades de los escenarios suman uno solo si los escenarios cubren todo el rango de resultados.* “If the scenarios represent only a sub-set of the possible outcomes on an investment, the probabilities will not add up to one.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 4)
- **Escenarios: probabilidades conjuntas** — *Con dos factores, las probabilidades se asignan de forma conjunta a cada combinación y deben cuadrar por filas y columnas.* “These probabilities represent joint probabilities; the probability of Airbus going ahead with a large jet that will directly compete with the Boeing Super Jumbo in a high-growth Asian economy is 12.5%.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 6)
- **Escenarios: rango de valores** — *Lo más útil del análisis de escenarios es el rango de valores entre escenarios, que da una idea del riesgo del activo.* “The most useful information from a scenario analysis is the range of values across different scenarios, which provides a snap shot of the riskiness of the asset; riskier assets will have values that vary more across scenarios and safer assets will have manifest more value stability.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 7)
- **Escenarios: calidad de los escenarios** — *La clave es que los escenarios sean realistas y cubran el espectro de posibilidades.* “Not only do the outlined scenarios have to be realistic but they also have to try to cover the spectrum of possibilities.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 8)
- **Escenarios: límite del riesgo continuo** — *Los cortes entre escenarios son subjetivos cuando el riesgo es continuo, y un cambio mínimo cambia de escenario.* “the reality is that the cut-off points that we used of 4% and 7% are subjective; thus a growth rate of 7.1% will put us in the high growth scenario but a growth rate of 6.9% will yield an average growth scenario.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 9)
- **Escenarios: doble cuenta del riesgo** — *Rechazar una inversión por la probabilidad de quedar bajo el punto de equilibrio, cuando el valor esperado ya está ajustado por riesgo, cuenta dos veces el mismo riesgo.* “Since the expected value is already risk adjusted, this would represent a double counting of potentially the same risk or risk that should not be a factor in the decision in the first place (because it is diversifiable).” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 9)
- **Árboles de decisión: riesgo secuencial** — *Los árboles sirven cuando el activo debe superar una serie de pruebas y un fallo puede significar pérdida total del valor.* “In some projects and assets, risk is not only discrete but is sequential. In other words, for the asset to have value, it has to pass through a series of tests, with failure at any point potentially translating into a complete loss of value.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 9)
- **Árboles de decisión: plegar el árbol** — *Se pliega hacia atrás: promedio ponderado en nodos de azar y mejor rama en nodos de decisión.* “If the node is a chance node, the expected value is computed as the probability weighted average of all of the possible outcomes. If it is a decision node, the expected value is computed for each branch, and the highest value is chosen (as the optimal decision).” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 12)
- **Árboles de decisión: límite de riesgos concurrentes** — *Los árboles no modelan bien los riesgos que afectan al activo a la vez; los de escenarios sí.* “In particular, decision trees are best suited for risk that is sequential; the FDA process where approval occurs in phases is a good example. Risks that affect an asset concurrently cannot be easily modeled in a decision tree.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 18)
- **Árboles de decisión: valores esperados sin ajustar** — *El valor esperado ponderado por probabilidades no está ajustado por riesgo; solo el riesgo diversificable justificaría la tasa libre de riesgo.* “The probability-weighted expected value that we obtain is not risk adjusted. The only rationale that can be offered for using a risk free rate is that the risk embedded in the uncertain outcomes is asset-specific and will be diversified away, in which case the risk adjusted discount rate would be the riskfree rate.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 20)
- **Árboles de decisión: doble cuenta con tasas altas** — *No debe usarse una tasa alta que ya refleje la probabilidad de fracaso si esa probabilidad está en el árbol.* “We do have to be careful about making sure that we don’t double count for risk in decision trees by using risk-adjusted discount rates that are set high to reflect the possibility of failure at the earlier phases.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 20)
- **Árboles de decisión: ejemplo de capital riesgo** — *Descontar con la tasa objetivo del 35% dentro de un árbol cuenta el riesgo dos veces y da un valor mucho menor.* “Using the target rate of 35% as the discount rate in a decision tree would lead to a drastically lower value, because risk would have been counted twice.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 21)
- **Simulaciones: riesgo continuo** — *Los escenarios y árboles tratan el riesgo discreto; las simulaciones el continuo.* “If scenario analysis and decision trees are techniques that help us to assess the effects of discrete risk, simulations provide a way of examining the consequences of continuous risk.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 22)
- **Simulaciones: correlaciones** — *Las variables correlacionadas, como inflación y tasas, deben tratarse: variar solo una o modelar la correlación explícitamente.* “While both inputs may be critical in determining value, they are likely to be correlated with each other; high inflation is usually accompanied by high interest rates.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 25)
- **Simulaciones con restricciones** — *Una simulación sirve para riesgo cuando se fija una restricción cuya violación crea costos muy altos o la desaparición de la empresa.* “To use simulations as a tool in risk analysis, we have to introduce a constraint, which, if violated, creates very large costs for the firm and perhaps even causes its demise.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 33)
- **Simulaciones: quiebra y costos indirectos** — *El flujo descontado apenas recoge el riesgo de incumplir deuda; la simulación permite modelar la distress.* “The possibility and potential costs of not being able to meet debt payments is considered only peripherally in the discount rate. In reality, the costs of not meeting contractual obligations can be substantial.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 35)
- **Simulaciones: basura entra, basura sale** — *Las distribuciones deben basarse en datos y análisis, porque la simulación da buenos gráficos aun con inputs aleatorios.* “It is worth noting that simulations yield great-looking output, even when the inputs are random. Unsuspecting decision makers may therefore be getting meaningless pictures of the risk in an investment.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 36)
- **Simulaciones: correlaciones inestables** — *Si las correlaciones cambian con el tiempo, modelarlas es mucho más difícil.* “To the extent that correlations between input variables change over time, it becomes far more difficult to model them.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 37)
- **Simulaciones: tasa ajustada por riesgo** — *Los flujos de una simulación son flujos esperados, no ajustados por riesgo, y se descuentan a tasa ajustada por riesgo.* “Looking across simulations, the cash flows that we obtain are expected cash flows and are not risk adjusted. Consequently, we should be discounting these cash flows at a risk-adjusted rate.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 37)
- **Simulaciones: penalizar dos veces** — *Rechazar un activo por su desviación estándar simulada tras descontar con tasa más alta lo penaliza dos veces.* “If you now proceed to reject asset B, because the standard deviation is higher across the simulated values, you would be penalizing it twice.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 38)
- **Comparación: probabilidades que suman uno** — *En escenarios las probabilidades pueden sumar menos de uno; en árboles y simulaciones suman uno y permiten un valor esperado.* “the sum of the probabilities of the scenarios we examine in scenario analysis can be less than one, whereas the sum of the probabilities of outcomes in decision trees and simulations has to equal one.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 39)
- **Comparación: correlación entre riesgos** — *Los escenarios recogen correlaciones de forma subjetiva; los árboles las manejan mal.* “In scenario analysis, we can deal with correlations subjectively by creating scenarios that allow for them; the high (low) interest rate scenario will also include slower (higher) economic growth. Correlated risks are difficult to model in decision trees.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 39)
- **Comparación: riesgos nuevos** — *Ante riesgos nuevos e impredecibles se recurre al análisis de escenarios pese a su carácter subjetivo.* “Thus, it should come as no surprise that when confronted with new and unpredictable risks, analysts continue to fall back on scenario analysis, notwithstanding its slapdash and subjective ways of dealing with risk.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 40)
- **Complemento del valor ajustado por riesgo** — *El análisis de escenarios siempre complementa al valor ajustado por riesgo; con flujos esperados no se usa la tasa libre de riesgo.* “Scenario analysis, on the other hand, will always be a complement to risk adjusted value, since it does not look at the full spectrum of possible outcomes.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 40)
- **Conclusión: simulaciones** — *Las simulaciones dan la evaluación más completa del riesgo: un valor esperado y una distribución de valores.* “Simulations provide the most complete assessments of risk since they are based upon probability distributions for each input (rather than a single expected value or just discrete outcomes).” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 42)

## Ups and Downs

- **Idea central** — *El valor de estas empresas depende de una variable macro (precio de la materia prima o economía), y los resultados del último año reflejan la fase del ciclo; extrapolarlos produce malas valoraciones.* “Since both commodity prices and economies move in cycles, the biggest problem we face in valuing companies tied to either is that the earnings and cash flows reported in the most recent year are a function of where we are in the cycle, and extrapolating those numbers into the future can result in serious misvaluations.” (Ups and Downs, p. 2)
- **Remedio general** — *No predecir el próximo ciclo: normalizar ganancias y flujos sobre el ciclo.* “We argue that trying to forecast the next cycle is not only futile but dangerous and that it is far better to normalize earnings and cash flows across the cycle.” (Ups and Downs, p. 3)
- **Cíclica vs. materias primas** — *Las cíclicas siguen a la economía; las de materias primas producen un bien cuyo precio determina sus resultados (dos grupos distintos).* “The second group of companies are commodity companies that derive their earnings from producing commodities that may become inputs to other companies in the economy (oil, iron ore) or be desired as investments in their own right (gold, platinum, diamonds).” (Ups and Downs, p. 3)
- **Precio de la materia prima** — *La empresa de materias primas es tomadora de precios: sus ingresos siguen al precio de mercado.* “Unlike firms in many other businesses, commodity companies are, for the most part, price takers. In other words, even the largest oil companies have to sell their output at the prevailing market price.” (Ups and Downs, p. 5)
- **Riesgo de quiebra** — *Los shocks macro obligan a vigilar la supervivencia incluso de empresas sanas.* “it is easy to see why we have to be more concerned about distress and survival with cyclical and commodity firms than with most others.” (Ups and Downs, p. 6)
- **Valor terminal y recursos finitos** — *En materias primas, los recursos finitos pueden limitar el supuesto habitual de crecimiento perpetuo del valor terminal.* “When valuing commodity companies, this will not only play a role in what our forecasts of future commodity prices will be but may also operate as a constraint on our normal practice of assuming perpetual growth (in our terminal value computations).” (Ups and Downs, p. 6)
- **Peligro de extrapolar un pico** — *Tomar como base un año de pico sobrevalora la empresa.* “If the base year is at the peak or close to the peak of a cycle, and we use the numbers from that year as the basis for valuation, we will over value companies.” (Ups and Downs, p. 7)
- **Peligro de extrapolar un valle** — *Tomar como base un año de valle infravalora sistemáticamente la empresa.* “If the base year represents the bottom or trough of a cycle and we use the earnings from that year to value companies, we will consistently under estimate their values.” (Ups and Downs, p. 7)
- **Tasa de descuento a lo largo del ciclo** — *Tipo libre de riesgo y prima de riesgo cambian con el ciclo, así que fijar el coste de capital del último año es un error.* “However, riskfree rates and risk premiums will change over the economic cycle, with the former decreasing and the latter increasing, as the economy slows.” (Ups and Downs, p. 7)
- **Fijar el último año** — *Bloquear ganancias, reinversión y coste de capital del último año lleva a valoraciones erróneas.* “In summary, locking in earnings, reinvestment and cost of capital numbers from the most recent year for a cyclical or commodity firm is a recipe for erroneous valuations.” (Ups and Downs, p. 8)
- **Prever el ciclo** — *Construir previsiones de ciclos futuros añade ruido y puede empeorar la estimación.* “if we cannot forecast economic and commodity price cycles with any accuracy, and even professional forecasters admit that their crystal balls are hazy even in the short term, trying to build in long term forecasts of cycles not only adds noise to the valuation and may actually undercut the quality of the overall estimate.” (Ups and Downs, p. 9)
- **Normalización selectiva: incompleta** — *Normalizar solo las ganancias es un error: hay que normalizar también rentabilidad del capital, reinversión y coste de financiación.* “To do normalization right, we have to carry it to its logical extreme. In addition to normalizing earnings, we also have to normalize return on capital, reinvestment and cost of financing.” (Ups and Downs, p. 10)
- **Normalización selectiva: crecimiento** — *Si se normalizan las ganancias al alza y además se usa un crecimiento optimista de recuperación, se cuenta el crecimiento dos veces.* “In effect, we are double counting growth, once by normalizing earnings and again by using a higher growth rate.” (Ups and Downs, p. 11)
- **Ventana de normalización (5–10 años)** — *La ganancia normalizada se estima normalmente como media de un periodo de 5 a 10 años.* “Normalized earnings, estimated usually by looking at average earnings over a period (5 to 10 years), are also commonly used with multiples to value companies in these sectors.” (Ups and Downs, p. 11)
- **Estabilidad falsa** — *Subir en la cuenta de resultados da estabilidad, pero el inversor reclama beneficios y flujos, no ventas ni EBITDA.* “While the search for a more stable base makes sense, we have to recognize that investors cannot lay claim to revenues or EBITDA and that they ultimately still care about the bottom line (earnings and cash flows).” (Ups and Downs, p. 11)
- **Qué es un año normal** — *Año normal: punto medio del ciclo en cíclicas; precio que refleja oferta y demanda en materias primas; hay subjetividad.* “If we are talking about cyclical companies, a normal year would be one that represents the mid-point of the cycle, where the numbers are neither puffed up nor deflated by economic conditions. With commodity companies, a normal year would be one where commodity prices reflect the intrinsic price of the commodity, reflecting the underlying demand and supply.” (Ups and Downs, p. 14)
- **Longitud del ciclo** — *El promedio debe cubrir un ciclo completo, y los ciclos van de 2–3 a más de 10 años.* “At least in theory, the averaging should occur over a period long enough to cover an entire cycle. In economic cycles, even in mature economies like the United States, can range from short periods (2-3 years) to very long ones (more than 10 years).” (Ups and Downs, p. 14)
- **Media absoluta: defecto de escala** — *Promediar cifras absolutas infravalora si la empresa creció durante el periodo.* “In other words, using the average earnings over the last 5 years as the normalized earnings for a firm that doubled its revenues over that period will understate the true earnings.” (Ups and Downs, p. 14)
- **Margen medio sobre ventas actuales** — *Promediar márgenes del ciclo y aplicarlos a las ventas más recientes resuelve el problema de escala.* “In effect, we can average profit margins over time, instead of net profits, and apply the average profit margin to revenues in the most recent period to estimate normalized earnings.” (Ups and Downs, p. 14)
- **Medias sectoriales** — *Útiles sin historia larga, con menos volatilidad, pero ignoran las particularidades de la empresa.* “The biggest advantage of the approach is that sector margins tend to be less volatile than individual company margins, but this approach will also fail to incorporate the characteristics (operating efficiencies or inefficiencies) that may lead a firm to be different from the rest of the sector.” (Ups and Downs, p. 15)
- **Ejemplo Toyota: margen histórico** — *El margen medio histórico aplicado a las ventas actuales recoge a la vez la escala actual y la calidad de la empresa.* “Averaging the pre-tax operating margin from 1998 to 2009 yields an average operating margin of 7.33%. Applying this margin to the revenues in 2009 yields a normalized operating income of 1,660.7 billion yen (7.33% of 22,661 billion yen), an estimate that captures both the larger scale of the firm today and its success in this business.” (Ups and Downs, p. 16)
- **Coste de capital normalizado** — *Se examina el coste de capital a lo largo del tiempo y, si es estable, se usa como coste de capital normalizado.* “We did examine the cost of capital for Toyota over time, and since neither the debt ratio nor the cost of capital has moved substantially over time, we will use this as the normalized cost of capital.” (Ups and Downs, p. 17)
- **Valor terminal normalizado** — *En estado estable el crecimiento se limita al tipo libre de riesgo y la inversión rinde su coste de capital.* “Since Toyota is already the largest automobile firm in the world, in terms of market share, we will assume that the firm is in stable growth, growing at 1.50% (capped at the riskfree rate) in perpetuity.” (Ups and Downs, p. 17)

## Financial Service Firms

- **Resumen: equity y dividendos** — *Los bancos y aseguradoras se valoran por el patrimonio (modelos de equity) y con dividendos reales o potenciales, no por flujo de caja libre de la empresa.* “We argue that financial service firms are best valued using equity valuation models, rather than enterprise valuation models, and with actual or potential dividends, rather than free cash flow to equity.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 1)
- **Resumen: flujos no estimables** — *El primer problema es que el flujo de caja de una financiera no se puede estimar bien porque inversión, circulante y deuda no están definidos.* “The first is that the cash flows to a financial service firm cannot be easily estimated, since items like capital expenditures, working capital and debt are not clearly defined.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 1)
- **Marco regulatorio: capital regulatorio** — *El regulador exige ratios de capital sobre el patrimonio contable para que la entidad no crezca más allá de sus medios.* “banks and insurance companies are required to maintain regulatory capital ratios, computed based upon the book value of equity and their operations, to ensure that they do not expand beyond their means and put their claimholders or depositors at risk.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 5)
- **Marco regulatorio: crecimiento y reinversión** — *Las hipótesis de crecimiento van ligadas a las de reinversión y deben pasar las restricciones regulatorias.* “From a valuation perspective, assumptions about growth are linked to assumptions about reinvestment. With financial service firms, these assumptions have to be scrutinized to ensure that they pass regulatory constraints.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 5)
- **Deuda y capital** — *Para una financiera la deuda es materia prima, no fuente de capital; por eso el coste de capital y el valor de la empresa pierden sentido.* “Rather than view debt as a source of capital, most financial service firms seem to view it as a raw material. In other words, debt is to a bank what steel is to a manufacturing company” (Financial Service Firms, p. 7)
- **Provisiones y calidad de la cartera** — *El beneficio depende de cuán prudente sea la entidad al provisionar: el banco agresivo muestra más beneficio en épocas buenas.* “A conservative bank will set aside more for loan losses, given a loan portfolio, than a more aggressive bank, and this will lead to the latter reporting higher profits during good times.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 7)
- **Apalancamiento y volatilidad del patrimonio** — *Con el patrimonio como una fracción pequeña del valor, pequeños cambios en el valor de los activos producen grandes oscilaciones del valor del patrimonio.* “Since equity is a sliver of the overall value of a financial service firm, small changes in the value of the firm’s assets can translate into big swings in equity value.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 8)
- **Reinversión difícil de definir** — *La inversión de una financiera es en intangibles y se contabiliza como gasto operativo, así que no se ve como inversión de capital.* “financial service firms invest primarily in intangible assets such as brand name and human capital. Consequently, their investments for future growth often are categorized as operating expenses in accounting statements.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 8)
- **Coste de capital engañoso** — *Tratar todos los depósitos y préstamos como deuda da costes de capital irrealmente bajos.* “If we combine these high debt ratios with the low costs of debt, we will end up with costs of capital that are unrealistically small – 4% or lower for many banks.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 9)
- **Beneficios como flujo: reparto del 100 %** — *No se puede descontar el beneficio con crecimiento positivo: crecer exige reinvertir en capital regulatorio, ni siquiera un banco maduro puede repartirlo todo.* “That is why reinvestment has to include investments in regulatory capital, acquisitions and other such investments that banks need to make to continue to grow. That is also why even mature banks with low growth rates cannot afford to pay out 100% of their earnings as dividends.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 10)
- **Dividendos observados no siempre sostenibles** — *Usar los dividendos actuales infravalora a quien reparte de menos y sobrevalora a quien reparte de más y se financia con acciones nuevas.* “If we value the former, using actual dividends paid, we will under value them, since we will build in the current practice of paying too little into their cashflows in perpetuity. If we value the latter, using actual dividends paid, we will over value them.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 11)
- **Wells Fargo: dividendo en riesgo** — *Un modelo simple con el dividendo y el crecimiento históricos sobrevalora cuando el beneficio se hunde y el dividendo tendrá que recortarse.* “It is likely that Wells Fargo will have to cut dividends to reflect the deterioration of earnings.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 11)
- **Valor contable no es valor de mercado** — *Que los activos estén a valor de mercado no dispensa de valorarlos: los errores del mercado y de los modelos internos quedan en el patrimonio contable.* “Analysts who equate book value to market value, because of marking to market, are not only abandoning their responsibility for estimating value but can make significant errors for two reasons.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 12)
- **Pérdida de información sobre calidad de inversión** — *Con activos realmente a mercado, el ROE tiende al coste del capital propio y ya no distingue buenas de malas inversiones.* “In fact, if assets are truly marked to market, the return on equity on every assets should be equal to the cost of equity; there is nothing to distinguish firms making good investments from those making bad investments.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 13)
- **Regulación y riesgo de la cartera** — *Confiar en el regulador y comparar PER sin controlar el riesgo de la cartera de préstamos hace parecer baratos a los bancos más arriesgados.* “When analysts compare the price earnings ratios of banks, for instance, and do not control for the risk of the loan portfolios of these banks, they are assuming that all banks are equally risky. Not surprisingly, riskier banks will look cheaper in this comparison.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 13)
- **Patrimonio, no empresa** — *Sin deuda identificable no se puede descontar flujos previos a la deuda al coste de capital; se valora el patrimonio al coste del capital propio.* “Estimating cash flows prior to debt payments at a weighted average cost of capital is problematic, when debt and debt payments cannot be easily identified, which, as we argued earlier, is the case with financial service firms.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 14)
- **Reparto, crecimiento y capital regulatorio** — *Repartir más implica retener menos y crecer menos, y el patrimonio contable debe mantenerse en porcentajes fijados de la actividad.* “banks and insurance companies have to maintain equity (in book value terms) at specified percentages of their activities. When a company is paying out more in dividends, it is retaining less in earnings; the book value of equity increases by the retained earnings.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 18)
- **Cambios regulatorios y riesgo** — *Los betas del sector reflejan la regulación vigente; si esta cambia, hay que reconsiderar el riesgo de todos los bancos.* “When significant changes are expected to regulation, we should consider the potential impact on betas across the board.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 18)
- **Crecimiento = ROE × retención** — *El crecimiento esperado del beneficio es el ROE por la parte no repartida.* “Expected growth in earnings = Return on equity * (1 – Dividend Payout ratio) For instance, a bank that payout out 60% of its earnings as dividends and earns a return on equity of 12% will have an expected growth rate in earnings of 4.8%.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 19)
- **El ROE a largo plazo como variable clave** — *Lo decisivo en un banco no es el dividendo ni el crecimiento sino el ROE de largo plazo, que fija el reparto y el crecimiento coherentes.* “At the risk of hyperbole, the key number in valuing a bank is not dividends, earnings or growth rate, but what we believe it will earn as return on equity in the long term.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 19)
- **ROE implícito de un dividendo y crecimiento dados** — *Si se supone un crecimiento perpetuo con un reparto dado, el ROE exigido puede ser inalcanzable y hay que bajar crecimiento o dividendo.* “To deliver a growth rate of 4% a year forever, the return on equity that Wells Fargo would have to deliver on it’s new investment is 16.67%.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 20)
- **Normalizar desde el patrimonio** — *En vez de partir de beneficios y dividendos inestables de crisis, se parte del patrimonio contable y de un ROE normalizado.* “We started with the book value of equity of $47,628 million that Wells Fargo reported at the end of 2008, and estimated what earnings and dividends would be at a normalized return on equity.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 20)
- **ROE y coste del capital propio como motores** — *El valor depende de dos entradas, ROE y coste del capital propio; los escenarios con peor rentabilidad y más riesgo cambian mucho el resultado.* “The two inputs that will determine the value of equity at Wells Fargo are the return on equity and the cost of equity.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 21)
- **Flujo al accionista y capital regulatorio** — *Cada euro de capital regulatorio retenido permite un múltiplo de préstamos nuevos; el beneficio no repartido financia ese capital.* “For instance, a bank that has a 5% equity capital ratio can make $100 in loans for every $5 in equity capital. When this bank reports net income of $15 million and pays out only $5 million, it is increasing its equity capital by $10 million.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 22)
- **Modelo de rendimiento excedente** — *El valor del patrimonio es el capital propio invertido más el valor actual de los excesos de rentabilidad esperados.* “Value of Equity = Equity Capital invested currently + Present Value of Expected Excess Returns to Equity investors” (Financial Service Firms, p. 22)
- **ROE por debajo del coste: valor bajo contable** — *Quien gana solo el coste del capital vale su patrimonio contable; quien gana menos vale menos.* “A firm that invests its equity and earns just the fair-market rate of return on these investments should see the market value of its equity converge on the equity capital currently invested in it. A firm that earns a below-market return on its equity investments will see its equity market value dip below the equity capital currently invested.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 23)
- **Límites del patrimonio contable** — *El valor contable del patrimonio puede subestimar el capital invertido por recompras y cargos extraordinarios.* “Notwithstanding this, the book value of equity can be affected by stock buybacks and extraordinary or one-time charges.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 23)
- **Valoración por activos: límites** — *Valorar la cartera y restar la deuda no da valor al crecimiento ni a los excesos de rentabilidad futuros.* “First, it does not assign any value to expected future growth and the excess returns that flow from that growth.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 26)
- **Regulación como fuente de rentabilidad o límite** — *La regulación puede permitir excesos de rentabilidad al limitar la competencia o restringirlos al impedir entrar en negocios.* “Finally, regulatory considerations and constraints overlay financial firm valuations. In some cases, regulatory restrictions on competition allow financial service firms to earn excess returns and increase value.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 33)
- **Controlar los fundamentales al comparar** — *Al comparar múltiplos hay que controlar riesgo, crecimiento, flujos y calidad de los préstamos.* “In making these comparisons, we have to control for differences in fundamentals – risk, growth, cash flows, loan quality – that affect value.” (Financial Service Firms, p. 33)
