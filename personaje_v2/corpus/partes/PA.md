- **Mejor/peor caso: factibilidad** — *Si cada input se lleva a su extremo a la vez, el caso puede no ser factible; hay que respetar las relaciones entre inputs.* “The problem with this approach is that it may not be feasible; after all, to get the high revenue growth, the firm may have to lower prices and accept lower margins.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 2)
- **Mejor/peor caso: riesgo de quiebra** — *El peor caso sirve para juzgar si una inversión podría llevar a la empresa con deuda a incumplir.* “Thus, a firm that has significant debt obligations may use the worst-case outcome to make a judgment as to whether an investment has the potential to push them into default.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 2)
- **Mejor/peor caso: utilidad limitada** — *Un rango muy amplio entre mejor y peor caso no ayuda a decidir si el precio actual es razonable.* “In general, though, best case/worse case analyses are not very informative. After all, there should be no surprise in knowing that an asset will be worth a lot in the best case and not very much in the worst case.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 2)
- **Escenarios: pocos factores** — *Los escenarios se construyen sobre dos o tres factores críticos.* “In general, analysts should focus on the two or three most critical factors that will determine the value of the asset and build scenarios around these factors.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 3)
- **Escenarios: cuántos** — *Pocos escenarios son más fáciles de estimar que muchos; el número depende de cuán distintos son y de cuánto se pueden prever sus flujos.* “Thus, estimating cash flows under each scenario will be easier if the firm lays out five scenarios, for instance, than if it lays out 15 scenarios.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 3)
- **Escenarios: probabilidades y cobertura** — *Las probabilidades de los escenarios suman uno solo si los escenarios cubren todo el rango de resultados.* “If the scenarios represent only a sub-set of the possible outcomes on an investment, the probabilities will not add up to one.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 4)
- **Escenarios: probabilidades conjuntas** — *Con dos factores, las probabilidades se asignan de forma conjunta a cada combinación y deben cuadrar por filas y columnas.* “These probabilities represent joint probabilities; the probability of Airbus going ahead with a large jet that will directly compete with the Boeing Super Jumbo in a high-growth Asian economy is 12.5%.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 6)
- **Escenarios: rango de valores** — *Lo más útil del análisis de escenarios es el rango de valores entre escenarios, que da una idea del riesgo del activo.* “The most useful information from a scenario analysis is the range of values across different scenarios, which provides a snap shot of the riskiness of the asset; riskier assets will have values that vary more across scenarios and safer assets will have manifest more value stability.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 7)
- **Escenarios: calidad de los escenarios** — *La clave es que los escenarios sean realistas y cubran el espectro de posibilidades.* “Not only do the outlined scenarios have to be realistic but they also have to try to cover the spectrum of possibilities.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 8)
- **Escenarios: límite del riesgo continuo** — *Los cortes entre escenarios son subjetivos cuando el riesgo es continuo, y un cambio mínimo cambia de escenario.* “the reality is that the cut-off points that we used of 4% and 7% are subjective; thus a growth rate of 7.1% will put us in the high growth scenario but a growth rate of 6.9% will yield an average growth scenario.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 9)
- **Escenarios: doble cuenta del riesgo** — *Rechazar una inversión por la probabilidad de quedar bajo el punto de equilibrio, cuando el valor esperado ya está ajustado por riesgo, cuenta dos veces el mismo riesgo.* “Since the expected value is already risk adjusted, this would represent a double counting of potentially the same risk or risk that should not be a factor in the decision in the first place (because it is diversifiable).” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 9)
- **Árboles de decisión: riesgo secuencial** — *Los árboles sirven cuando el activo debe superar una serie de pruebas y un fallo puede significar pérdida total del valor.* “In some projects and assets, risk is not only discrete but is sequential. In other words, for the asset to have value, it has to pass through a series of tests, with failure at any point potentially translating into a complete loss of value.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 9)
- **Árboles de decisión: plegar el árbol** — *Se pliega hacia atrás: promedio ponderado en nodos de azar y mejor rama en nodos de decisión.* “If the node is a chance node, the expected value is computed as the probability weighted average of all of the possible outcomes. If it is a decision node, the expected value is computed for each branch, and the highest value is chosen (as the optimal decision).” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 12)
- **Árboles de decisión: límite de riesgos concurrentes** — *Los árboles no modelan bien los riesgos que afectan al activo a la vez; los de escenarios sí.* “In particular, decision trees are best suited for risk that is sequential; the FDA process where approval occurs in phases is a good example. Risks that affect an asset concurrently cannot be easily modeled in a decision tree.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 18)
- **Árboles de decisión: valores esperados sin ajustar** — *El valor esperado ponderado por probabilidades no está ajustado por riesgo; solo el riesgo diversificable justificaría la tasa libre de riesgo.* “The probability-weighted expected value that we obtain is not risk adjusted. The only rationale that can be offered for using a risk free rate is that the risk embedded in the uncertain outcomes is asset-specific and will be diversified away, in which case the risk adjusted discount rate would be the riskfree rate.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 20)
- **Árboles de decisión: doble cuenta con tasas altas** — *No debe usarse una tasa alta que ya refleje la probabilidad de fracaso si esa probabilidad está en el árbol.* “We do have to be careful about making sure that we don’t double count for risk in decision trees by using risk-adjusted discount rates that are set high to reflect the possibility of failure at the earlier phases.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 20)
- **Árboles de decisión: ejemplo de capital riesgo** — *Descontar con la tasa objetivo del 35% dentro de un árbol cuenta el riesgo dos veces y da un valor mucho menor.* “Using the target rate of 35% as the discount rate in a decision tree would lead to a drastically lower value, because risk would have been counted twice.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 21)
- **Simulaciones: riesgo continuo** — *Los escenarios y árboles tratan el riesgo discreto; las simulaciones el continuo.* “If scenario analysis and decision trees are techniques that help us to assess the effects of discrete risk, simulations provide a way of examining the consequences of continuous risk.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 22)
- **Simulaciones: correlaciones** — *Las variables correlacionadas, como inflación y tasas, deben tratarse: variar solo una o modelar la correlación explícitamente.* “While both inputs may be critical in determining value, they are likely to be correlated with each other; high inflation is usually accompanied by high interest rates.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 25)
- **Simulaciones con restricciones** — *Una simulación sirve para riesgo cuando se fija una restricción cuya violación crea costos muy altos o la desaparición de la empresa.* “To use simulations as a tool in risk analysis, we have to introduce a constraint, which, if violated, creates very large costs for the firm and perhaps even causes its demise.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 33)
- **Simulaciones: quiebra y costos indirectos** — *El flujo descontado apenas recoge el riesgo de incumplir deuda; la simulación permite modelar la distress.* “The possibility and potential costs of not being able to meet debt payments is considered only peripherally in the discount rate. In reality, the costs of not meeting contractual obligations can be substantial.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 35)
- **Simulaciones: basura entra, basura sale** — *Las distribuciones deben basarse en datos y análisis, porque la simulación da buenos gráficos aun con inputs aleatorios.* “It is worth noting that simulations yield great-looking output, even when the inputs are random. Unsuspecting decision makers may therefore be getting meaningless pictures of the risk in an investment.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 36)
- **Simulaciones: correlaciones inestables** — *Modelar correlaciones solo funciona si son estables y predecibles.* “To the extent that correlations between input variables change over time, it becomes far more difficult to model them.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 37)
- **Simulaciones: tasa ajustada por riesgo** — *Los flujos de una simulación son flujos esperados, no ajustados por riesgo, y se descuentan a tasa ajustada por riesgo.* “Looking across simulations, the cash flows that we obtain are expected cash flows and are not risk adjusted. Consequently, we should be discounting these cash flows at a risk-adjusted rate.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 37)
- **Simulaciones: penalizar dos veces** — *Rechazar un activo por su desviación estándar simulada tras descontar con tasa más alta lo penaliza dos veces.* “If you now proceed to reject asset B, because the standard deviation is higher across the simulated values, you would be penalizing it twice.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 38)
- **Comparación: probabilidades que suman uno** — *En escenarios las probabilidades pueden sumar menos de uno; en árboles y simulaciones suman uno y permiten un valor esperado.* “the sum of the probabilities of the scenarios we examine in scenario analysis can be less than one, whereas the sum of the probabilities of outcomes in decision trees and simulations has to equal one.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 39)
- **Comparación: correlación entre riesgos** — *Los escenarios recogen correlaciones de forma subjetiva; los árboles las manejan mal.* “In scenario analysis, we can deal with correlations subjectively by creating scenarios that allow for them; the high (low) interest rate scenario will also include slower (higher) economic growth. Correlated risks are difficult to model in decision trees.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 39)
- **Comparación: riesgos nuevos** — *Ante riesgos nuevos e impredecibles se recurre al análisis de escenarios pese a su carácter subjetivo.* “Thus, it should come as no surprise that when confronted with new and unpredictable risks, analysts continue to fall back on scenario analysis, notwithstanding its slapdash and subjective ways of dealing with risk.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 40)
- **Complemento del valor ajustado por riesgo** — *El análisis de escenarios siempre complementa al valor ajustado por riesgo; con flujos esperados no se usa la tasa libre de riesgo.* “Scenario analysis, on the other hand, will always be a complement to risk adjusted value, since it does not look at the full spectrum of possible outcomes.” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 40)
- **Conclusión: simulaciones** — *Las simulaciones dan la evaluación más completa del riesgo: un valor esperado y una distribución de valores.* “Simulations provide the most complete assessments of risk since they are based upon probability distributions for each input (rather than a single expected value or just discrete outcomes).” (Probabilistic Approaches, p. 42)
