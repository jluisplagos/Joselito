- **Excess returns y valor** — *El crecimiento sin retornos en exceso no crea valor; es el eje de los modelos de valoración recientes.* “more recent iterations of these models have noted that growth unaccompanied by excess returns creates no value.” (Measuring Returns, p. 2)
- **Retorno frente a coste de capital** — *Una empresa que gana más de lo que le cuesta el capital obtiene retornos en exceso y cotiza con prima.* “A firm that generates higher returns on an investment than it costs it to raise capital for that investment is earning excess returns and will trade at a premium over a firm that does not earn excess returns.” (Measuring Returns, p. 5)
- **Crecimiento = reinversión × ROIC** — *El crecimiento esperado de la empresa es la tasa de reinversión por el retorno del capital nuevo.* “In firm valuation models, the expected growth rate is a product of the return on capital invested in new assets and the proportion of operating income reinvested back into the business (reinvestment rate)” (Measuring Returns, p. 5)
- **Qué número manda en la valoración** — *El retorno sobre el capital importa más que el coste de capital.* “we can safely conclude that the key number in a valuation is not the cost of capital that we assign a firm but the return earned on capital that we attribute to it.” (Measuring Returns, p. 6)
- **Definición de ROIC** — *El ROIC mide el retorno sobre el capital invertido en una inversión, con resultado operativo después de impuestos en el numerador.* “The return on capital or invested capital in a business attempts to measure the return earned on capital invested in an investment.” (Measuring Returns, p. 7)
- **Capital del año anterior** — *El denominador es el capital invertido al cierre del año previo y el numerador el resultado operativo del año corriente.* “The final is the timing difference; the capital invested is from the end of the prior year whereas the operating income is the current year’s number.” (Measuring Returns, p. 7)
- **Capital contable y no de mercado** — *Se usa valor contable porque el de mercado incluye activos de crecimiento que aún no generan resultado operativo.* “The market value of equity includes the expected value of growth assets, which cannot generate operating income today.” (Measuring Returns, p. 9)
- **Neto de caja** — *La caja se resta del capital porque su interés no forma parte del resultado operativo.* “The reason we net out cash is to be consistent with the use of operating income as our measure of earnings. The interest income from cash is not part of operating income.” (Measuring Returns, p. 9)
- **Capital al inicio del periodo** — *Las inversiones hechas durante el año no producen resultado ese año, por eso se divide por el capital inicial.* “Using the rationale that investments made during the course of a year will generally not start generating earnings during the year, we divide the operating income for the year by the capital invested at the beginning of the year.” (Measuring Returns, p. 10)
- **ROE frente a ROIC y la caja** — *El ROE contable es un retorno compuesto de todos los activos, caja incluida, a diferencia del ROIC.* “The return on equity for a company is therefore a composite return on all of its assets – cash and operating.” (Measuring Returns, p. 12)
- **Capitalización de I+D: vida útil por sector** — *La vida amortizable de la I+D depende del sector: larga en farmacia, corta en software.* “To illustrate, research and development expenses at a pharmaceutical company should have fairly long amortizable lives, since the approval process for new drugs is long. In contrast, research and development expenses at a software firm, where products tend to emerge from research much more quickly should be amortized over a shorter period.” (Measuring Returns, p. 19)
- **Ajuste del resultado operativo por I+D** — *Se suma el gasto de I+D y se resta la amortización del activo de investigación.* “First, the R&D expenses that were subtracted out to arrive at the operating income are added back to the operating income, reflecting their re-categorization as capital expenses. Next, the amortization of the research asset is treated the same way that depreciation is and netted out to arrive at the adjusted operating income.” (Measuring Returns, p. 20)
- **Efecto de capitalizar I+D** — *Capitalizar I+D tiende a bajar los retornos que parecían altos antes del ajuste.* “Firms that report high returns on a pre-adjustment basis are more likely to see their returns go down, post-adjustment.” (Measuring Returns, p. 21)
- **Arrendamientos operativos como deuda** — *Los arrendamientos operativos son compromisos contractuales equivalentes a deuda y deben tratarse como tal.* “Operating lease commitments look very much like debt commitments insofar as firms as contractually obligated to make them.” (Measuring Returns, p. 31)
- **Partidas no recurrentes** — *El resultado usado para calcular retornos debe reflejar operaciones continuadas, sin partidas únicas o extraordinarias.* “As a general rule, the income that is used to compute returns on equity and capital should reflect continuing operations and should not include any items that are one-time or extraordinary.” (Measuring Returns, p. 37)
- **Recompras y ROE** — *Dividendos y recompras elevan el ROE al reducir el patrimonio contable más que el resultado.* “In general, the return on equity of a firm that pays a large dividend or buys back stock will increase after the transaction because the book value of equity will decrease disproportionately, relative to the net income.” (Measuring Returns, p. 39)
- **Goodwill y activos de crecimiento** — *Del goodwill, solo el valor de los activos de crecimiento debe salir del capital invertido; el resto se queda.* “After all, a company cannot be asked to generate a return on investments it has not thought about yet. The other elements of goodwill, though, should remain part of capital invested.” (Measuring Returns, p. 41)
- **Participaciones minoritarias** — *Las participaciones minoritarias no afectan al resultado operativo, así que su inversión se excluye del capital invertido.* “Minority holdings in other companies will not affect operating income but the investments in these holdings should not be included as part of invested capital.” (Measuring Returns, p. 46)
- **Volatilidad del ROE frente al ROIC** — *El ROE es más volátil que el retorno sobre capital porque el interés es un coste fijo.* “The returns on equity are more volatile than the returns on capital: Equity earnings will be more volatile than operating earnings, largely because interest expenses comprise a fixed cost.” (Measuring Returns, p. 49)
- **Retorno marginal frente a medio** — *Las empresas invierten primero en lo mejor, así que el retorno marginal suele quedar por debajo del medio.* “If we begin with the proposition that firms invest in their best projects first and move down their investment schedule to less and less attractive investments, the marginal return on capital (or equity) should be lower than the average return for most firms.” (Measuring Returns, p. 51)
- **Dell: el medio engaña** — *Un retorno medio alto no implica que las inversiones nuevas ganen lo mismo.* “Assuming that Dell has significant new investments that will continue to earn these returns would be not only foolhardy but lead to too high an estimate of value for the company.” (Measuring Returns, p. 52)
- **Competencia erosiona el exceso** — *Los retornos en exceso atraen competidores que los presionan a la baja.* “While this excess return may be justified using historical data or industry averages, the presence of these returns will undoubtedly draw in new competitors over time, putting downward pressure on these returns over time.” (Measuring Returns, p. 56)
- **Competencia perfecta** — *En competencia perfecta el exceso desaparece de inmediato; hacen falta barreras para que persista.* “In a perfectly competitive market place, excess returns will not persist for more than an instant in time and all firms will earn zero excess returns.” (Measuring Returns, p. 56)
- **Ventaja competitiva duradera** — *Cuanto más fuerte y sostenible la ventaja, mayor y más largo el retorno en exceso.* “Competitive advantages that are strong and sustainable generate larger excess returns for longer periods than more fleeting or weaker competitive advantages.” (Measuring Returns, p. 57)
- **Retornos más persistentes que el crecimiento** — *El crecimiento revierte rápido a la media; el ROIC puede mantenerse alto mucho tiempo.* “The McKinsey study suggests that while revenue growth tends to revert quickly to average levels, returns on invested capital can remain high for extended periods.” (Measuring Returns, p. 59)
- **Horizonte de la ventaja** — *La duración del periodo de exceso puede venir fijada por la vida explícita de la ventaja, como una patente.* “In some cases, the length of the short term and transition periods will be determined by the fact that the competitive advantage has an explicit life.” (Measuring Returns, p. 60)
- **ROIC = coste de capital en estabilidad** — *Si el retorno iguala el coste de capital, subir el crecimiento estable no cambia el valor.* “If the return on capital is equal to the cost of capital, increasing the stable growth rate will have no effect on value.” (Measuring Returns, p. 61)
- **Tope del exceso perpetuo** — *Las empresas maduras ganan como mucho 2-3 puntos sobre su coste de capital a largo plazo; ese es el techo.* “At best, mature firms have earned 2-3% over their cost of capital over long periods and this should represent a cap on excess returns in perpetuity.” (Measuring Returns, p. 63)
- **Suelo del retorno terminal** — *En el valor terminal el retorno no debe quedar por debajo del coste de capital.* “Thus, it seems prudent to assume that the return on capital (equity) will, at the minimum, be equal to the cost of capital (equity) in the terminal value calculation.” (Measuring Returns, p. 63)
- **Conclusión: los excesos decrecen** — *Los retornos altos pueden durar unos años, pero decaen con el tamaño y la competencia.* “Thus, firms that have generated high returns in the past may continue to make these returns for the next few years, but the excess returns will start decreasing as firms become larger and competition increases.” (Measuring Returns, p. 64)
