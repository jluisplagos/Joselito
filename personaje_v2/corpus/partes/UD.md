- **Idea central** — *El valor de estas empresas depende de una variable macro (precio de la materia prima o economía), y los resultados del último año reflejan la fase del ciclo; extrapolarlos produce malas valoraciones.* “Since both commodity prices and economies move in cycles, the biggest problem we face in valuing companies tied to either is that the earnings and cash flows reported in the most recent year are a function of where we are in the cycle, and extrapolating those numbers into the future can result in serious misvaluations.” (Ups and Downs, p. 2)
- **Remedio general** — *No predecir el próximo ciclo: normalizar ganancias y flujos sobre el ciclo.* “We argue that trying to forecast the next cycle is not only futile but dangerous and that it is far better to normalize earnings and cash flows across the cycle.” (Ups and Downs, p. 3)
- **Cíclica vs. materias primas** — *Las cíclicas siguen a la economía; las de materias primas producen un bien cuyo precio determina sus resultados (dos grupos distintos).* “The second group of companies are commodity companies that derive their earnings from producing commodities that may become inputs to other companies in the economy (oil, iron ore) or be desired as investments in their own right (gold, platinum, diamonds).” (Ups and Downs, p. 3)
- **Precio de la materia prima** — *La empresa de materias primas es tomadora de precios: sus ingresos siguen al precio de mercado.* “Unlike firms in many other businesses, commodity companies are, for the most part, price takers. In other words, even the largest oil companies have to sell their output at the prevailing market price.” (Ups and Downs, p. 5)
- **Riesgo de quiebra** — *Los shocks macro obligan a vigilar la supervivencia incluso de empresas sanas.* “it is easy to see why we have to be more concerned about distress and survival with cyclical and commodity firms than with most others.” (Ups and Downs, p. 6)
- **Valor terminal y recursos finitos** — *En materias primas, los recursos finitos pueden limitar el supuesto habitual de crecimiento perpetuo del valor terminal.* “When valuing commodity companies, this will not only play a role in what our forecasts of future commodity prices will be but may also operate as a constraint on our normal practice of assuming perpetual growth (in our terminal value computations).” (Ups and Downs, p. 6)
- **Peligro de extrapolar un pico** — *Tomar como base un año de pico sobrevalora la empresa.* “If the base year is at the peak or close to the peak of a cycle, and we use the numbers from that year as the basis for valuation, we will over value companies.” (Ups and Downs, p. 7)
- **Peligro de extrapolar un valle** — *Tomar como base un año de valle infravalora sistemáticamente la empresa.* “If the base year represents the bottom or trough of a cycle and we use the earnings from that year to value companies, we will consistently under estimate their values.” (Ups and Downs, p. 7)
- **Tasa de descuento a lo largo del ciclo** — *Tipo libre de riesgo y prima de riesgo cambian con el ciclo, así que fijar el coste de capital del último año es un error.* “However, riskfree rates and risk premiums will change over the economic cycle, with the former decreasing and the latter increasing, as the economy slows.” (Ups and Downs, p. 7)
- **Fijar el último año** — *Bloquear ganancias, reinversión y coste de capital del último año lleva a valoraciones erróneas.* “In summary, locking in earnings, reinvestment and cost of capital numbers from the most recent year for a cyclical or commodity firm is a recipe for erroneous valuations.” (Ups and Downs, p. 8)
- **Prever el ciclo** — *Construir previsiones de ciclos futuros añade ruido y puede empeorar la estimación.* “if we cannot forecast economic and commodity price cycles with any accuracy, and even professional forecasters admit that their crystal balls are hazy even in the short term, trying to build in long term forecasts of cycles not only adds noise to the valuation and may actually undercut the quality of the overall estimate.” (Ups and Downs, p. 9)
- **Normalización selectiva: incompleta** — *Normalizar solo las ganancias es un error: hay que normalizar también rentabilidad del capital, reinversión y coste de financiación.* “To do normalization right, we have to carry it to its logical extreme. In addition to normalizing earnings, we also have to normalize return on capital, reinvestment and cost of financing.” (Ups and Downs, p. 10)
- **Normalización selectiva: crecimiento** — *Si se normalizan las ganancias al alza y además se usa un crecimiento optimista de recuperación, se cuenta el crecimiento dos veces.* “In effect, we are double counting growth, once by normalizing earnings and again by using a higher growth rate.” (Ups and Downs, p. 11)
- **Ventana de normalización (5–10 años)** — *La ganancia normalizada se estima normalmente como media de un periodo de 5 a 10 años.* “Normalized earnings, estimated usually by looking at average earnings over a period (5 to 10 years), are also commonly used with multiples to value companies in these sectors.” (Ups and Downs, p. 11)
- **Estabilidad falsa** — *Subir en la cuenta de resultados da estabilidad, pero el inversor reclama beneficios y flujos, no ventas ni EBITDA.* “While the search for a more stable base makes sense, we have to recognize that investors cannot lay claim to revenues or EBITDA and that they ultimately still care about the bottom line (earnings and cash flows).” (Ups and Downs, p. 11)
- **Qué es un año normal** — *Año normal: punto medio del ciclo en cíclicas; precio que refleja oferta y demanda en materias primas; hay subjetividad.* “If we are talking about cyclical companies, a normal year would be one that represents the mid-point of the cycle, where the numbers are neither puffed up nor deflated by economic conditions. With commodity companies, a normal year would be one where commodity prices reflect the intrinsic price of the commodity, reflecting the underlying demand and supply.” (Ups and Downs, p. 14)
- **Longitud del ciclo** — *El promedio debe cubrir un ciclo completo, y los ciclos van de 2–3 a más de 10 años.* “At least in theory, the averaging should occur over a period long enough to cover an entire cycle. In economic cycles, even in mature economies like the United States, can range from short periods (2-3 years) to very long ones (more than 10 years).” (Ups and Downs, p. 14)
- **Media absoluta: defecto de escala** — *Promediar cifras absolutas infravalora si la empresa creció durante el periodo.* “In other words, using the average earnings over the last 5 years as the normalized earnings for a firm that doubled its revenues over that period will understate the true earnings.” (Ups and Downs, p. 14)
- **Margen medio sobre ventas actuales** — *Promediar márgenes del ciclo y aplicarlos a las ventas más recientes resuelve el problema de escala.* “In effect, we can average profit margins over time, instead of net profits, and apply the average profit margin to revenues in the most recent period to estimate normalized earnings.” (Ups and Downs, p. 14)
- **Medias sectoriales** — *Útiles sin historia larga, con menos volatilidad, pero ignoran las particularidades de la empresa.* “The biggest advantage of the approach is that sector margins tend to be less volatile than individual company margins, but this approach will also fail to incorporate the characteristics (operating efficiencies or inefficiencies) that may lead a firm to be different from the rest of the sector.” (Ups and Downs, p. 15)
- **Ejemplo Toyota: margen histórico** — *El margen medio histórico aplicado a las ventas actuales recoge a la vez la escala actual y la calidad de la empresa.* “Averaging the pre-tax operating margin from 1998 to 2009 yields an average operating margin of 7.33%. Applying this margin to the revenues in 2009 yields a normalized operating income of 1,660.7 billion yen (7.33% of 22,661 billion yen), an estimate that captures both the larger scale of the firm today and its success in this business.” (Ups and Downs, p. 16)
- **Coste de capital normalizado** — *Se examina el coste de capital a lo largo del tiempo y, si es estable, se usa como coste de capital normalizado.* “We did examine the cost of capital for Toyota over time, and since neither the debt ratio nor the cost of capital has moved substantially over time, we will use this as the normalized cost of capital.” (Ups and Downs, p. 17)
- **Valor terminal normalizado** — *En estado estable el crecimiento se limita al tipo libre de riesgo y la inversión rinde su coste de capital.* “Since Toyota is already the largest automobile firm in the world, in terms of market share, we will assume that the firm is in stable growth, growing at 1.50% (capped at the riskfree rate) in perpetuity.” (Ups and Downs, p. 17)
- **Precio normalizado de la materia prima** — *La normalización en materias primas se construye sobre un precio normalizado, que afecta también a reinversión y financiación.* “As it moves up and down, it not only impacts revenues and earnings but also reinvestment and financing costs. Consequently, normalization with commodity companies has to be built around a normalized commodity price.” (Ups and Downs, p. 18)
- **Precios de mercado a plazo** — *Para quitar la opinión propia sobre el precio, usar los precios de mercado (futuros) de la materia prima.* “If we want to remove our views of commodity prices from valuations of commodity companies, the safest way to do this is to use market-based prices for the commodity in our forecasts.” (Ups and Downs, p. 19)
- **Crecimiento adaptativo** — *Normalizar a largo plazo pero dejar que los resultados sigan el ciclo en el corto, usando el crecimiento para volver a la normalidad.* “One compromise solution is to assume normalization in the long term, but to allow earnings to follow the current cycle for the short term, and to use the growth rate as a mechanism to bring us back to normalcy.” (Ups and Downs, p. 23)
- **Múltiplos de ganancias normalizadas** — *Tras normalizar, la mayor incertidumbre de las ganancias se refleja en múltiplos más bajos.* “In particular, we should expect to see firms that have more risky earnings trade at lower multiples of normalized earnings than firms with more stable earnings.” (Ups and Downs, p. 28)
- **Múltiplos en el ciclo** — *Los múltiplos de ganancias de estas empresas son mínimos en el pico y máximos en el valle.* “In particular, the multiples of earnings for cyclical and commodity firms will bottom out at the peak of the cycle and be highest at the bottom of the cycle.” (Ups and Downs, p. 28)
- **Coherencia al comparar** — *Hay que medir las ganancias de forma coherente en todas las empresas comparadas.* “First, it is critical that we stay consistent in how we measure earnings with commodity and cylcial companies. If we decide to use trailing earnings, we should do so for all companies.” (Ups and Downs, p. 30)
- **Volatilidad del precio y valor** — *El valor depende del precio y también de su volatilidad esperada, vía opciones de reservas sin desarrollar.* “The value of a commodity company is a function of not only the price of the commodity but also the expected volatility in that price.” (Ups and Downs, p. 38)
